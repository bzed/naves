# Changelog

All notable changes to this project are documented here. The format follows
[Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/).

## [Unreleased]

### Fixed

- A road is held to a junction it crosses without a joint. The height solve held a road to a junction only at a joint of
  its strip that lies in the junction's patch (or at the nearest free joint of a piece within 13 m); a strip that runs
  through the patch between a bridge deck and a joint that is held to another junction or is the deck, with no joint of
  its own there, was held to nothing, so it stayed on the ground while the junction's plane stood above it (240795460 at
  Berchtesgaden: a 2.4 m break and a 2.5 m `road_conflict` at its junction, 6.2 m from the deck end, patch 5.0 m). The
  junction's plane now holds the strip's interpolated height where it runs nearest the junction inside the patch, by a
  row with the weight of the grade limits, so that a plane the grade limits do not allow shows as `road_grade` or
  `road_not_drivable`, not as a break. A junction in the patch of a deck's end is left to the rules of the deck.

### Changed

- A bridge deck is held to the junction at its end, and a step there is reported. A run of bridge pieces is one level
  deck with one height, which the height solve held to a junction from the deck's middle: a deck whose middle lay more
  than about 10 m from the junction at its end (a 25 m metal bridge, a 10 m panel grid ending 5.4 m from an overlay) was
  not held at all, the roads on the two sides of the joint got different heights (1.5 m at Bergwerkstrasse) and the
  ground over the deck stood in the lane. Now the junction rules (the patch, the end of a chain, a meeting with an
  earlier road, and the lean of the junction's plane) measure from the end of the deck that faces the junction, and a
  chain that starts at the end of another chain's deck takes the deck's height. The shoulder ring of any road (the bridge's own included)
  inside a deck's end guard and width takes at most the deck's surface (it held the ground over the deck before; the road's
  core is as it was). A deck with a junction at each end that want different heights keeps a step at one of them, and
  that is the new finding `bridge_end_step` (the deck and the junction or road it joins differ by more than 0.5 m; also in
  the MCP rule list). On the Berchtesgaden example at `terrain_size = 2048`: `bridge_buried` 20 to 6 (the four sites of the
  game check: three are free of ground, 48450084 keeps 0.81 m from a wide road that leaves the end node at an acute angle),
  `bridge_end_step` 3 (0.5, 0.6 and 0.8 m, not reported before; 2.4 m at 240795460 is gone with the junction hold above; a deck end is compared with the junction the solve holds it to and, for a deck inside a chain, with the junction's plane where the road runs through its patch), `road_conflict` 905 to 899, `road_not_drivable` 186 to 185 and
  `road_misses_terrain` 4292 to 4285. **Worse:** Silbergstraße (1341010238) has 0.27 m of ground in its deck (0.14 m before), road pieces missing the terrain by more than 2 m are 156 (150 before), and `bridge_clearance` 0 to 1 and `road_grade` 3 to 4 are both at bridge 299428041,
  whose road below starts at the bridge's end node: it had an unreported 5.7 m step and now starts at the deck's
  height and keeps 2.3 m of the 4.5 m clearance, with a sharp crest. The deck stays level; pitched decks are not part of this.
- Bridges, as measured on the Berchtesgaden example at `terrain_size = 2048` (`docs/research/2026-11-18-bridges.md`):
  79 of 84 OpenStreetMap bridges are built from a part (a bridge of at most 3 m is plain road on purpose; 65 were built
  before), 6 bridges have ground in their deck (`bridge_buried`, 64 spans before; 20 before a deck was held from its end), 3 solid parts stand on a road
  below (`bridge_blocks_road`, 8 before), four of the six bridges over roads are measured and keep 4.5 m, one (299428041) reaches 2.3 m (Silbergstraße, way 1341010238, is
  not measured, so its clearance is not shown), and the road findings are `road_misses_terrain` 4285 (14 more than
  before the ground work, 4271; the baseline is 4317), `road_conflict` 899 (the baseline is 900) and
  `road_not_drivable` 185 (the baseline is 189); `road_grade` is 4 and `bridge_clearance` 1, both one worse than before
  (see the next entry).
- **Breaking:** the ground under a bridge is shaped for the bridge, not left alone. Under every placed part of a bridge (a model between its ports, a step of a panel grid, from the placed deck and its height: a grid lengthened over a road, a model slid or grown, a grid that follows a bend) the terrain is cut to a ceiling of the deck's height less what hangs under it (the catalog's slices of 1 m) less `BRIDGE_AIR_M` = 1 m; the cut spreads sideways with the cutting slopes across the width of what is below the deck plus one and a half cells, so that the opening is open at both sides. Under what stands (end blocks, arch flanks, posts, the cross walls of `bridge_concrete3`) the cut is the same and never goes to the foot: the foot is only the lowest that another road's cutting may take the ground to, and nothing beside the deck has one. Where an end holds nothing back the ceiling rises to the road's height at the port. Within the end guard of a free end (a cell along the deck, `cell * (|ax| + |ay|)` for a deck turned against the grid; the whole of a deck shorter than two guards) the ground is under the deck but not cut for it, so that a terrain cell that straddles the port line does not slope the road's last piece down; beyond the guard ground under a deck is never raised, inside it the bridge's own road may hold or raise it up to its road height (the abutment's fill). The road a bridge is part of, up to the next junction at each end, holds nothing under its deck beyond the guard; a road above the underside of the deck claims a vertex under it beyond the guard only within its own width, not with its shoulder ring (inside the guard the ring stays, and a road beside the deck keeps its shoulder); inside the guard, ground above the deck's surface within the deck's width is cut down to the surface (never below it) unless another road's earthwork, its core or its shoulder ring, holds it; a road below keeps its core and the ground around it, and the core of another road is never changed. New findings `bridge_buried` (ground in the deck: more than 0.1 m above the underside of the deck beyond the guard, above its surface inside it, with the road or patch that holds it or "no road"; the message names the reference) and `bridge_post_floats` (a post, pier or wall foot more than 0.5 m above the shaped ground) in `validation_list`; a `road_conflict` that the cut under a deck causes says so. `conform_bridges` and `bridgeground::bridge_spans` in the library, `Conformed` has `buried` and `floating`. A step of a lengthened panel grid now starts where its placed panel does, not at the end of the junction piece the grid lies over (the first step used to fold back and leave the deck's end without a span). The old rule that left the ground under a span alone is gone.
- A bridge over a road is placed so that the road passes through its opening. For every road below a bridge (a crossing of the two lines; hits within the deck's and the road's half widths of a shared node are none, a road that leaves the node, loops and crosses further on is found there) the part carries the road's footprint (`width / sin(angle)`, the sine at least 0.01), a metre of margin, the skew of its centre line across the part and, for panels and plain decks, the run of the cutting's walls; 4.5 m at the centre line and 3 m over the footprint, and nothing that stands (end blocks, arch flanks, posts, the cross walls of `bridge_concrete3`) passes a road. The first that works is used: the part the table gives, slid along the bridge's line within its straight room (and the turn limit and the posts); the panel grid, lengthened into the road beside the bridge by exactly what each end lacks (a grid over a road below grows by at most 15 m at an end and as far as that road goes on, keeping a metre before a junction, another bridge, a tunnel or a dead end; an end where the road's footprint would still be outside the deck does not grow; a bridge of 3 m or less over a road is such a grid; it needs no straight room and follows the road's bend; a grid not over a road never grows; `bridge_concrete3` over a road becomes a panel grid); else the placement with the least missing and the new finding `bridge_blocks_road` (also in the MCP rule list) with the missing metres. `report.json` has `slid_m` and `below` per bridge. The clearance of the height solve keeps 4.5 m under the deck's deepest point over the road's centre line and 3 m over its footprint, not the deck's thickness over its longest opening. `bridge_blocks_road` is reported where a placed piece of the road below (or of a part split from it) runs under the placed deck; `report.json` says `placed` per road. New module `bridgefit`; `over_crossings` gives the angle.

- A bridge gets a straight lead on its own axis, and a missed heading is reported. Where the road beyond a bridge's end leaves it turned away from the deck, its last stretch before the bridge is made straight on the deck's axis (`2 R tan(turn/2)` metres, at least 4 m, R the tightest curve of the road's pieces), so that the road joins the deck instead of leaving a kink or a gap (the fit to a grid turned by 66 degrees gave up with a 37 m gap on the Berchtesgaden example); nothing is moved at a junction, at a node of bridges or where the road is too short. The steps of a bent grid run chord to chord on the heading of their chord. Where no lead is possible the road is fitted to the grid's start on its own heading, so that it closes in position, and the heading that is left is the new finding `road_kink` (more than 5 degrees off the deck's heading, reported only at the ends of bridges, with the angle and the site); `road_gap` and the moving of junctions count positions only, as before. A road leaves its OpenStreetMap line by up to the lead's length near a bridge (up to 12 m on the Berchtesgaden example). `build/report.json` says per bridge what was done at each end (`lead`) and where a short model lies (`part_start_m`).
- Panel grids and the short bridge models are laid. A bridge built from concrete panels is a real bridge: `count` panels along its own line, from its start to its end, each on the line's heading at its centre (a grid follows a bend), `abreast` side by side centred on the line, all scaled alike, crosswise (yaw is the heading plus 90 degrees) with the origin 0.12 m times the scale below the deck level (measured in the game). The grid is one level deck for the height solve, as thick as a panel at its scale, with no road under it; the two bridges that were plain road in the interim (Silbergstraße and the bridge at 2347, 6546 on the Berchtesgaden example) are bridges again. Panels are the only scaled objects of a bridge. A model lies from the start of its stretch whichever way a road is walked, a model shorter than its bridge is centred on it (it keeps to the end where another bridge meets it, so that decks of bridges that meet end exactly at their node). The Sakhal archive (`bridge_wood2`, `bridge_concrete3`) is unpacked for `binarize` when the map uses its models; a missing archive stops the build and names the model. A model shorter than its bridge lies in one place for the post check, the report (`part_start_m`) and the placement: centred, or reaching the node where other bridges meet it (between two such nodes it is not used and the bridge is a panel grid). A panel grid joins the road before and after it: the approach is fitted to the grid's first step (position and heading) and the road after it starts on the last step's heading; a corner the pieces cannot close is a `road_gap`. `build/report.json` has a `bridges` list and the build prints one line per OpenStreetMap bridge: what it was built from (fill, panels n x k at scale s, model x count) and why.
- An OpenStreetMap bridge is built from the part that fits its length, and only a model grows, only along a straight line. At most 3 m it is plain road on the terrain (unless it meets other bridges at a fork of bridges); from two thirds of the length of a bridge model of its road it gets the shortest such model that covers it (a chain of the longest for a longer bridge; `bridge_wood2` alone, never chained; `bridge_wood_25` only where the ground under each of its posts is within their reach); every other bridge is a grid of concrete panels that covers it exactly: panels along the bridge and side by side, all scaled alike (0.75 to 3) so that they make the bridge's length and come as near the road's width as they can. A model longer than the bridge grows into the road at both ends only where the bridge and that road turn by at most 5 degrees together and 1 m stays before a junction, another bridge or a dead end; otherwise the bridge is a panel grid. Bridge ways that follow each other are one bridge, with the id of the lower outer way (edits of the other ways no longer find their objects). No bridge is dropped, and the build keeps for every bridge what it became, why, and why its room ends at each end; the warning `bridge on <id>: no room for a bridge model between the junctions` is gone. Before, every bridge grew to its family's longest model around any bend (on the Berchtesgaden example a 3.9 m bridge became a 37 m deck with an end 13 m inside a hillside) and 19 of 84 were dropped. A bridge over a dip now keeps 1 m from the end of its stretch where the road beyond turns at once (more than 5 degrees) or is another bridge, and an OpenStreetMap bridge built from its own part gets no bridge over a dip. The new finding `bridge_not_built` reports an OpenStreetMap bridge laid as plain road because the install has no panels and no bridge model that fits it.
- Bridges are never scaled. A road gets the bridge models of its family (asphalt 1 and streets `bridge_metal_25`, asphalt 2 `bridge_stone_25`, asphalt 3 `bridge_stone_25` or `bridge_concrete3`, gravel and dirt tracks `bridge_wood_25` or `bridge_wood2`) that are at most 1 m narrower than its piece; wider is always fine. Which of them a bridge gets goes by its length (see below); a bridge over a dip gets the longest, at its own size.
- The road catalog also reads the concrete panels (`panel_concrete_1`, `_2`) from `addons/structures_roads.pbo`, `bridge_wood2` and `bridge_concrete3` from `sakhal/addons/structures_sakhal.pbo` and the concrete pipe from `addons/structures_industrial.pbo`, and measures per bridge model, on the faces of its Geometry LOD, what is below the deck in slices of 1 m: what hangs (the deck, the intrados of the stone arch) and what stands on the ground (the metal bridge's end blocks, the arch's abutments, the posts of the wooden bridge, the cross walls of `bridge_concrete3`), the solid parts exact to their ends, whether they hold the ground back at the ports, and where a road below would clear the bridge. The deck thickness for the clearance over a road below is the depth over the longest open stretch between the solid parts: 1.68 m for the metal bridge, 4.00 m for the stone arch (3.96 m before: the opening ends now where the arch flanks reach 4 m, and nothing in it is deeper), 2.60 m for the wooden one. Folders are found in any case; a missing archive or part is a warning and the build goes on; only the models used are taken out of the archives (`dayzmake inspect` and `cat`), and the model paths follow the prefix each archive names. The cached catalog is read again once. A bridge model whose faces cannot be used is open over its whole length with a warning; the warnings about borrowed deck thicknesses now come from the catalog, once per model.

- The heights of all roads and junctions are solved as one problem. Every height of every road and the height and tilt of every junction come out of one optimisation (a quadratic program, solved by the new dependency `clarabel`) instead of four steps that each fixed what the one before left over: the junction repair, the profile of each chain in turn, the settling of roads that share terrain cells and the lifts for bridges over roads. The heights no longer depend on the order of the roads. What cannot be kept (a grade over the limit of the family, a crest or a dip sharper than its vertical radius, a bridge that cannot clear the road below, junctions too far apart in height) is broken as little as possible and is reported as before. A road is steeper than its aim where the ground asks for it, at a cost, and not by a rule that gave way after 4 m; a junction is pulled to the height and tilt of the ground and moves as far as the roads around it need (no limit of 3 m; where two junctions are too steep for each other both move, and the rank of a road no longer decides which of them moves less; a junction on a bridge's road rises for the bridge as far as the grade limits and the vertical radius ask, with no cap, and the road below it may be lowered, so a large lift shows only as `road_far_from_ground` when it passes the earthwork allowance); a bridge deck has a bend at each end, which removes the ski jump at a deck end; roads side by side are tied to one height only where the ground under them differs by less than a terrain cell can hold plus a metre, so two legs of a hairpin on a steep flank keep their own heights. On the Berchtesgaden example at `terrain_size = 2048` (`road-analysis`, against the old pipeline on the same code): `road_grade` 1079 to 3 (it is now measured against the hard grade ceiling of the road's family, not against its aim, so part of the fall is the changed measure), `junction_too_steep` 8 to 0, `road_misses_terrain` 4625 to 4317, `road_conflict` 1025 to 900, `road_not_drivable` 208 to 189, the clearance of all four bridges over roads is reached (Silbergstrasse 0.5 m before) with no `road_grade` finding within 100 m of them, the roads are placed once instead of twice and the run takes 921 s instead of 1828 s. Bridges over dips are now rare by construction, not only on this example: a road follows the ground into a dip within its limits, so it no longer stands high over the dip a bridge would span (3 before on the example, none now). A build can now stop with "the road heights are not solved" if the solver reports no solution; the message names the size of the problem and the solver's status. The manual (getting started and troubleshooting) describes what is solved and what the findings mean. Library: `naves-core::profile::solve_profiles` and `solve_clearing` return a `Result`, and `solve_profiles_with`, `DeckFloor`, `junctions::repair` and `junctions::raise_floors` are gone.
- Joints of a road less than a metre apart are one height. The placement overlaps the 6.25 m straights of a long road, which put two joints a few centimetres apart; the profile solved them as two heights and the checks read a few centimetres as a steep grade and a sharp bend (`road_grade`). They are now one variable, except across a bridge deck or between two different junctions.
- Junctions on a slope tilt with their through road. A junction used to hold all its joints at one height (a level patch), so two junctions a few metres apart on a slope left no room for the rise (725 of the 1645 `road_grade` findings of the Berchtesgaden example). Now the junction's plane follows the original ground under its through road (a junction piece's own axis, the top road of an overlay, the road a chain end lies on), fitted along that road over 25 m or the patch radius either side, at most the cross fall aim of 8 percent (the plane's fall along the through road is the fall across every road that meets it) and with no fall across it: the joints of a junction piece, the ends and inward joints of the side roads and the terrain patch under a join, overlay or chain end on a road lie on it. A join of ending roads and a junction on a roundabout stay level or on the ring's plane. The check for `junction_too_steep` measures a link between the heights of its two held joints.
- A bridge over a road keeps 4.5 m clearance. Where a road tagged as a bridge crosses another road without a shared node, the underside of its deck (the deck's thickness is measured from the Geometry LOD of the bridge model; each bridge model has its own; a model whose Geometry LOD cannot be read takes the thickest deck of its type, else of all models, and the build warns with its name) keeps 4.5 m from the surface of the road below. The room is made in the same solve as all road heights: the bridge, its approaches and the junctions near it rise and the road below may be lowered into a cutting, each within its grade limits and vertical radius, and the roads within 50 m of such a crossing are not tied to the road below. Where the two together cannot make 4.5 m, the new finding `bridge_clearance` (also in the MCP rule list) says what was reached. The cached road catalog is read again once (the bridge models now carry their deck thickness). Arrays that the game's models store compressed (LZO, 1024 bytes or more) are now decompressed when a model is read (new dependency `lzokay-native`, decompression only).
- A bridge deck lies on its OpenStreetMap line and the road before it closes on the deck's port. The road used to end up to 2 degrees off the deck's heading and the deck was laid along that heading, so a long bridge drifted sideways (3.5 m over 100 m after a bend) and the road behind it started off its line. The deck now keeps the heading of its line; the road before it is fitted to the deck's start, and the deck slides at most 2.5 m along its line, in 0.25 m steps, the shortest slide first, until the road ends within 0.1 m and 1 degree of the deck's port. Where no such slide exists, or the road arrives more than 2.5 degrees off the line's chord (a bridge on a curve), the deck stays at its place and starts where the road ends, with the road's heading, as before (a deck slid by metres put one end over the gap: up to 10 m in an earlier version of this change).
- A road that cannot close against the port of a junction piece moves the junction. When a fit ends more than 0.5 m from its port (`road_gap`), the junction is tried at 6.25 m steps along its through road, up to 25 m each way; the first position that closes the road is taken, else the smallest gap if it beats the unmoved one. The network is placed again with the moved junctions and kept only if it has fewer gaps, loses no junction piece and adds no join without junction; where a through road's gap before a moved junction grew beyond 0.5 m, that move is taken back (one more placement), and if one still grows nothing is moved. Junctions keep 12.5 m from their neighbours, stay off bridges and on a roundabout ring stay where they are. Placement takes about three times as long.
- Roundabouts are built as rings on one tilted plane that follows the slope. A closed loop of `junction=roundabout` (or `circular`) roads is placed as a closed loop of arcs and straights with no junction piece on it; every road that joins it ends under its edge (the overlay rule), at the plane's height. The plane is fitted to the original ground under the ring's road (least squares, tilt limited to the grade ceiling of the ring's road) and the terrain under the ring's road and beside it is shaped to it (the ground inside a ring keeps its height), so that a ring on a steep street needs no flat bench. A ring tighter than the tightest arc of its surface (10 m for the game's roads; most mini roundabouts at a small scale) becomes a plain crossing at its centre (a junction piece or the overlay rule). Open or cut loops stay ordinary roads.
- A road that OpenStreetMap draws twice is placed once. Two roads that run along each other for at least 25 m, closer than half the narrower road's width and within 15° of each other, are one road: the higher one (better surface, then the longer, then the lower way id) stays and the part of the other that runs along it is not placed; where the lower road goes on it ends on the kept road by the overlay rule. Each dropped part is a new finding, `road_duplicate_dropped` (also in the MCP rule list). Paths are not compared; a bridge is compared only with bridges (and a tunnel with tunnels) and is dropped whole, never cut. Cut pieces get the ids `<id>` and `<id>/rest<n>`.
- Where the game has no junction piece for a pair of road types, one road now lies on top of the other instead of the two meeting in a heap: the higher road (asphalt 1, asphalt 2, street, asphalt 3, gravel, dirt track; the longer one among equals) runs on, a lower road ends just under its edge (or is interrupted and starts again under the other edge where it crosses), without an end cap, 5 cm below the higher road's surface. A higher road that ends on a lower one lies on top of it. These places are no longer `join_without_junction` findings and their overlap is no `road_conflict`; the finding remains for crossings where all roads end. A piece of road shorter than half its width between two such cuts is left out.

### Fixed

- The meeting grade (6 %) applies only where a road really ends on another. It applied to every piece of a road within its half width and 12.5 m of any other road's end on the map, also where that end lay beside the road without reaching it, at another height: under the Silbergstrasse bridge on the Berchtesgaden example, the end of a short street at the junction above held five pieces of the road below, 12 to 17 m away, to 6 %. Now the other road's end has to lie on the road (within a metre of its edge), as for the heights.
- A road is cut under an overlay only where the two higher roads cross it. Two higher roads that left a lower road on the same side (a fork onto a lesser road) cut it on both sides of the node and left a hole of about 3 m in it; now the lower road runs on and the higher roads end on it.
- A road stays one chain through a bend at a node with only two roads. Two roads of one surface that meet alone at a node (the end of a bridge cut from a way, or an OpenStreetMap way that only changes bridge, tunnel or layer there) were continuations only if they bent less than 45 degrees; a bridge that began in a bend split the road, and the part before it ended free and overlapped the next without a finding. Now they continue each other up to a bend of 150 degrees (two ends drawn into one node at a sharper angle stay apart); nodes with three or more roads keep the 45 degree rule for choosing the through road. Warnings and `road_gap` findings of a run that does not begin at the first link of its chain (after a tunnel) now name their own stretch, not another.
- A bridge over a dip needs straight road before and after it. A bridge could start at the end of a hairpin (the Kehlsteinstrasse loop), where the road before it could not reach the deck and ended 5 m and 63 degrees short without a finding. At each end of the bridge every point of the road within 12.5 m outside it (also across a change of surface) must now lie within 10 degrees of the model's chord there, and the road over 4 m inside it within 20 degrees. A bridge that passes the 2 m straightness test but fails this is moved along the dip in 0.5 m steps (as far as it still covers the dip, the shorter move first); where no position passes, the dip is filled. Bridges of OpenStreetMap ways are not touched.
- A crossing of two roads of different surfaces gets a junction piece more often: where the game has an X junction only with the roles swapped (the street crossing an Asf1 road has `kr_x_asf1_city` only), that piece is used, turned by 90 degrees, where the through road's fit reaches the crossing. T crossings are never swapped (the Berchtesgaden example has one such crossing and it still has no junction piece, its fit ends 3.3 m from the crossing).
- Roads are drawn in the game. `binarize` could not open the road models, so a normal build had world files without any road piece shown. `naves build` now unpacks the game's `structures_roads.pbo` onto binarize's `P:` drive for the run (and removes it afterwards; the models are never in the mod), and a build fails with the model names when binarize still cannot open a road model, or when it finds no road directions in a world with roads.
- The DayZ client no longer crashes a few seconds after it enters a world with a fine grid. The client takes a surface tile as its unit of terrain detail only when the tile is 256 to 1024 m long; with shorter tiles (terrain cells under 4 m, such as the 6.4 km map at 2048 that was the default) it asks for a terrain texture that is never made. `naves build` (CLI, GUI and MCP) now refuses grids whose terrain cells are not 4 to 16 m long and names a `terrain_size` that fits, a new project gets the finest grid with cells of 4 m or more (1024 on a 6.4 km map), and the Berchtesgaden example grew to a 16.4 km area at half scale (an 8.2 km map) so that it stays at 2048 with cells of 4 m (the added area lies mostly to the south and west; the satellite images have to be fetched again).
- The MCP server uses rmcp 3.9.0, which fixes RUSTSEC-2026-0462; the dependency requirement now enforces it.
- Starting an external tool that was written or replaced moments earlier no longer fails with "text file busy": the kernel releases a closed file's write permission a moment after close, and the spawn now waits that moment out (up to one second).

### Changed

- dayzmake 2.0.0 or newer replaces armake2 as the packing, signing and image tool (<https://github.com/bzed/dayzmake>); Náves runs `dayzmake --version` once and refuses an older version. The setting is `tools.dayzmake` and the variable `DAYZMAKE`; an old `tools.armake2` key and the `ARMAKE2` variable are still read as fallbacks (the setting is saved under the new key only; the variable is deprecated). The road catalog cache stays valid. Packaging: `debian/control` now names dayzmake, for the next `debian/changelog` entry.
- The built-in CA bundle (webpki-roots) is updated from 1.0.11 to 1.0.12.
- The GUI now uses Rerun's design system (the Inter font, light and dark themes that follow the system, modern widgets) — the look and feel of the whole editor changed.
- The palette renders its rows with the design system's list items (hover, selection and focus come with them).
- The settings window is a modal of the design system: a title bar with a close button, and it keeps the keyboard and the pointer away from the map behind it.
- Build: naves-ui uses strum and strum_macros 0.28 (Rerun's re_ui 0.42.0 uses 0.26).
- Build: re-port naves-ui to Rerun's re_ui 0.42.0 on egui 0.37.
- Build: vendor Rerun's design system (from rerun_ui_kit's re_ui 0.38.1 extraction, MIT OR Apache-2.0) as the workspace crate naves-ui; not yet used by the GUI.
- Build: the Rust toolchain pin moved from 1.95 to 1.99 (required by the vendored design system).

### Added

- Bridges over dips. Where a road stands more than 3 m above the original ground over a run (a dip) and the dip is at least 8 m deep, it gets a bridge, or a chain of bridges end to end, when a model fits (the dip at least half a model long, whole models laid evenly over it clear of junctions and dead ends, the road within 2 m of straight over each model); otherwise, and for every dip between 3 and 8 m deep, a dry dip is filled as before and a dip over a stream, river, canal or lake is left and reported by the new rule `road_over_water` (also in the MCP rule list and the manual's troubleshooting section). The build now reads `waterway=stream`, `river` and `canal` lines from the OpenStreetMap files for this. **When a dip gets a bridge the roads are placed a second time, so the road part of the build takes about twice as long.** The ids of the new bridge objects are `road/<stretch>/bridge<n>/<slot>`, so an override on the pieces of a stretch after the first bridge over a dip becomes an orphan (it is kept and reported); the pieces before it keep their ids.
- Every OpenStreetMap bridge is built (see Changed: the part that fits its length). A bridge model shorter than 1 m, or with a width that is not positive, is never chosen.
- Bridges are chosen by surface: gravel and dirt roads get the wooden models, the paved roads the others.
- The new rule `road_far_from_ground` reports a road piece planned further from the original ground than fits its terrain: 30 m on gentle ground, up to 100 m where the ground across the road has a side slope of 0.60 or more (a cut "Cut 40.0 m deep; 30 m fit this ground" or an embankment). The rule is in the MCP rule list and the manual's troubleshooting section; nothing about the terrain or the roads changes.
- The terrain follows the road heights: the ground under a junction without a junction piece is levelled to the junction's height (outside the width of every road there), the uphill side of a road on ground that rises more than 35 % across it is a near-vertical face one cell wide (a player can no longer always walk off a road uphill on steep ground), and two roads or two legs of one road at different heights that come closer than their level strips each keep their own ground, with a face between them; only roads whose widths overlap are still a `road_conflict`. Where roads at different heights are less than about three cells apart more pieces now miss the terrain slightly (either road can), and more footpaths beside a face are reported as `path_too_steep`.
- Roads meet at the height of their junction: every junction gets one height, the median of the original ground within 10 m, and every road that reaches it (the through road at its junction piece, the side road at its end) is held to that height, so errors are no longer inherited from road to road. Junctions too close together for their heights both move, and what cannot be kept is the new rule `junction_too_steep`. Gravel and dirt tracks no longer take the 6 % grade of a meeting. The manual's "Roads" and troubleshooting sections and the MCP rule list name the new rule.
- The road profile keeps its grades over the run between the joints of a chain (not the length of a piece), smooths joints that are close together as one, and keeps the grade limits between two junction heights. A junction holds as little road as it can: one joint where a road passes it, the end where a road ends, both joints of a junction piece, and only the roads that belong to the junction (a parallel street or a road above it is not held). A junction is pulled to its ground and moves as far as the roads around it need; a link that cannot be kept within its grade limit is reported as `junction_too_steep`. The vertical radius is kept across a junction height. A link between two junctions is the road between the joints that hold their heights. More `road_grade` findings are reported than before: the grades are measured over the true run of each piece, and the junction heights are fixed.
- Roads that run closer together than the terrain grid can tell apart (hairpin legs, streets side by side, junction arms) are brought to the height one terrain cell can hold between them, in the same solve as all other heights, where the ground under them lets it. This replaces the earlier switchback pass.
- Level 4, Roads and paths, in the GUI: the layers **Roads** (true width, white casing), **Paths** (dashed) and **Drivable** (green, amber, red with cross ticks, grey), and **Terrain changes** under Terrain (cut and fill); a panel with the numbers of the network; click a road for its model, surface, grade and where it was generated from; what is wrong with the roads in Problems, with positions. The roads are generated in the background when a project with OpenStreetMap files opens; the road layers wait for them. A missing tool is named in the panel, with a button that opens the settings.
- MCP: `render_topdown` takes `layers` (`terrain_changes`, `too_steep_to_walk`, `roads`, `paths`, `drivable`), and the new tools `roads_stats` and `validation_list` (optionally for one rule) report the generated roads.
- `naves build` builds roads and paths from the OpenStreetMap files in `sources.osm`: `highway` ways become the game's own road pieces (asphalt, streets, gravel and dirt, junctions, end caps, bridges) laid along the OSM line, with the terrain shaped to fit them (benches on valley sides, embankments, cuttings; held to a slope limit, reaching up to 60 m), the road beds and footpaths painted on the ground, and the objects written to the world file. The pieces are read from the DayZ Server install (`addons/structures_roads.pbo`) through armake2 and cached per user; no model is copied into the mod. The result is checked on the final terrain, and findings (a road that cannot be driven, a piece in the air, a path too steep to walk, a road in a lake, roads that meet without a junction, tunnels left out) go into the build warnings and `build/report.json`. **The drivable and walkable limits are provisional**, pending measurement in the game. Edits go in `edits/overrides.toml` and survive a rebuild. A navigation mesh made earlier no longer fits the new terrain; the build warns. New manual section "Roads".
- A command palette (Ctrl+P) in the GUI: type words, Enter does it. It lists the levels, the level's tools and the build, and picking an entry is the same as pressing its shortcut.
- A settings window in the GUI (the Settings button in the dock, or F2) with Keyboard, Map and External tools. Every shortcut can be changed; a key already in use asks before it is replaced; Esc, Enter and F1 cannot be changed and Tab and Shift+Tab cannot be assigned. The Map tab sets the wheel direction, how strongly the other levels fade, the interface scale, whether the stack unfolds with animation, and the base map and layers a project opens with. External tools shows each path and where it comes from. Everything is saved at once in the per-user `settings.toml`; a damaged file is shown and never overwritten. New manual page "Settings".
- Per-user settings in `settings.toml` in Náves' configuration folder (`$XDG_CONFIG_HOME/naves`): the `[tools]` table names the paths of armake2, Steam, DayZ Tools, DayZ, DayZ Server and Proton. An environment variable still wins; the settings come before the search. `naves build`, `naves mcp` and the GUI use them, a path that does not exist is named, and a damaged file is a warning and is left alone. New: `DAYZ_SERVER` is documented.
- The GUI's bottom dock has the tabs Build and Problems. Problems lists the warnings and the error of the last build, the notes of the imagery merge and a setting that was not saved; a row with a position (and F8 and Shift+F8) takes the map there, and diamonds mark them on the map.
- Click in the GUI map to read a point in the active level (position, latitude and longitude, height and slope in Terrain; the ground class in Ground), Alt+click to pick across levels, Esc to clear, F to frame it and Shift+F to frame the whole map.
- Switchable map layers in the GUI, under the active level in the stack, starting with **Too steep to walk** (hatching over ground steeper than 60°, a provisional limit). H hides the active level, Shift+H shows the other levels in full; they are drawn at 40 % otherwise. New `naves_core::render::render_layer`.
- Keyboard shortcuts in the GUI, from one table: the digits 0 to 2 go to a level, Ctrl+B builds, F1 lists the keys that work in the level you are in, Esc closes the list. While a text field has the keyboard, no single key fires. The table already holds the final set of keys of the editor; a key whose action is not built yet does nothing. New manual page "Keyboard shortcuts".
- The GUI is built around the stack of levels: Project, Terrain and Ground (keys 0 to 2), each with its own left panel, a band in the level's colour on top of the map and a status line below it. The base maps (Relief, Slope, Height, Ground, Imagery) moved to the right column; "Hillshade" is called Relief and "Surface" Ground there. The panels fold on small windows.
- The GUI sets the imagery brightness (`[imagery] brightness`), and the Imagery map (GUI and MCP) shows the imagery with it, as the build makes it.
- The GUI lists the imagery sources for the area of the project and fetches them in the background (with progress and cancel), shows the files in their order with up, down and remove, accepts files of your own once they have a credit and a licence, and shows what the merge found out about each file.
- `naves imagery fetch <id>` (and the MCP tool `imagery_fetch`) downloads imagery from a catalogue source for the area of a project into its `imagery` folder and lists the files with their licence in `naves.toml`: mosaics from a WMS in tiles, Sentinel-2 scenes from a STAC search (the clearest ones of the last two summers, or of the dates given), cropped to the map. `naves build` still never uses the network. `make ca-bundle` (CI weekly and at every release) checks that the bundled CA list is the newest.
- `naves imagery list` (and the MCP tool `imagery_options`) shows which imagery sources exist for the area of a project, with pixel size, licence and a NON-COMMERCIAL mark; a user file `imagery-sources.toml` in the configuration folder can add sources. New manual chapter "Imagery".
- An **Imagery** overlay in the GUI map and in the MCP `render_topdown` tool shows the merged imagery (ground without imagery is checkered). The GUI lists the imagery files with their licences, says "Non-commercial map" when a file that touches the map is non-commercial, and shows the merge's notes in the log.
- `naves build` paints the satellite images of the terrain from the GeoTIFF files in `sources.imagery`: files that overlap are matched in colour and merged by sharpness with soft edges, and `[imagery] brightness` sets the brightness. A map that uses a non-commercial file says so in `ATTRIBUTION.txt` (mod and mission), in `mod.cpp` and in the build warnings; the credits of the files used are written to `ATTRIBUTION.txt`, and the mod folder now holds a `mod.cpp` with the name of the map.
- Project file: `sources.imagery` lists satellite or aerial GeoTIFFs with their attribution and licence (`non_commercial` is required where the licence says NC), and `[imagery] brightness` sets how bright they are drawn.
- `naves build` paints the ground from ESA WorldCover land cover and OpenStreetMap water, rock and built-up areas (`sources.land_cover`, `sources.osm`); the slope and altitude rules apply only where neither says anything. The build warns when the files do not cover the map or an extract cuts polygons, and writes `ATTRIBUTION.txt` (Copernicus, WorldCover, OpenStreetMap as used) into the mod folder.
- The GUI shows the build's warnings in its log. OpenStreetMap extracts are merged before areas are built, so a polygon that every extract cuts at its border is whole, and only rings on the map are counted in the skipped-rings warning.
- A **Surface** overlay in the GUI map (with a legend) and in the MCP `render_topdown` tool shows the ground classes; the GUI lists the land cover and OpenStreetMap files.
- The terrain has four ground layers (grass, rock, forest, soil); the ground of each map cell is decided in one place (`SurfaceGrid`).
- Project file `naves.toml`.
- Copernicus GLO-30 GeoTIFF DEM input.
- MCP server `naves mcp`: `project_get`, `project_set`, `heightmap_stats`, `render_topdown` and `build` for AI clients over stdio.
- GUI (`naves gui`): project settings that save to `naves.toml`, a pannable top-down map (hillshade, slope, height) and a build button with a live log.
- Project file: `sources.land_cover` (ESA WorldCover) and `sources.osm` (OpenStreetMap extracts) list the data that will paint the ground surfaces.
- `naves new` creates a project, `naves build` builds the signed mod and the server mission.
- Binarize worlds with DayZ Tools under Proton (sandboxed, with a timeout); the build checks for armake2,
  DayZ Tools and Proton up front and names the variable to set.
- World `config.cpp` and server mission generation (the player spawns in the middle of the map). The world
  inherits Livonia's sky, weather and terrain materials by reference and packs a `ce/` folder from the installed
  server mission; the game client needs both.
- Navigation mesh support: the world config has a `Navmesh` class (inherited agent and link types, seed in the map
  centre) and `naves build` packs `<project>/navmesh/<world>.nm` when it exists; the manual describes how to make
  the mesh with DayZDiag and Flare Navgen.
- Procedural surfaces: a hillshaded satellite image, a grass/rock mask (rock above 35° slope and above a real
  altitude of 2100 m) and a tile material for every terrain tile.

### Changed

- A new project has a terrain grid of 2048 (`land_size = 256`) instead of 1024, and the Berchtesgaden example is at 2048. A project file that names a size keeps it. `land_size` has no default in a project file: a file that names only `terrain_size` is refused with an error that names `land_size` (it was never paired with a default). The example's build through binarize takes about 400 s and gives a 233 MB PBO. **Known problem in the game: no road piece is drawn, because `binarize` cannot open the road models** (see `examples/berchtesgaden/README.md`).
- The drivable limits of tracks follow a car at its best and a 4WD: a gravel track is not drivable above 40 % and a dirt track above 50 % (before 30 % and 28 %); asphalt and streets keep 30 %. Tracks in the mountains are not expected to be drivable by an ordinary car. Estimates, not driven in the game.
- Road alignment, measured on the Berchtesgaden example with all of the above: the road that cannot be driven is 5.64 km long at 1024 and 5.05 km at 2048 (27.5 and 21.4 km before the alignment fixes), `road_conflict` 749 and 1130 findings, `road_misses_terrain` 4604 and 4015, `road_grade` 1640 and 1409. More pieces miss the terrain slightly with the final rules than with the earlier ones, while the conflicts fell. The numbers and what is left are in `docs/research/2026-11-10-road-alignment.md`, section 7.9.
- Earthworks follow the hillside: a cutting is 45° and an embankment 34° on gentle ground and both get steeper with the natural slope, up to 63° and 45°. A cutting in a steep hillside can be too steep to walk up; the downhill side can always be left on foot.
- A road gives up its grade aim before it leaves the ground, up to the ceiling of its family (nine tenths of what vehicles manage). Such pieces are amber (**Steep**), not not drivable.
- A junction piece is level, at its junction's height. The 6 % grade at a meeting applies only to a road between a free end and a junction, on asphalt and streets (gravel and dirt tracks keep their own limits, and between two junction heights the grade limits of the road apply), and a road is gentler in a tight bend so that its inner edge stays within the family's aim.
- A road's profile is limited by a vertical radius per family (600 m on `asf1` down to 60 m on a dirt track) instead of one change of grade per joint, so a short piece no longer bends more per metre than a long one; the grade excesses are measured on the joints of the solved profile.
- The road limits are in one place (`naves_core::limits`): the steepest grade a car climbs is 30 % on asphalt and streets (was 35 %); gravel and dirt tracks have their own limits, 40 % and 50 % (see the entry on the drivable limits of tracks), with aims of 18 % and 25 %; and a road may be tilted across by up to 20 %, with 8 % as the aim (was one hard limit of 5 %). A road between its family's aim and those limits is **Steep** (amber) instead of not drivable. The limits are estimates from real vehicles, not measured in the game yet.
- The GUI's look: boxes, radio buttons and fields have an outline that shows on the panel, selected rows are a light green that ink reads on, tabs are marked by a bar instead of a filled button, and keyboard focus is a green ring (white or ink on a coloured level row). The shortcut sheet (F1) is centred and has no shadow.
- The GUI map moves with Space + drag or the middle mouse button; the left button no longer pans.
- `naves.toml` with an option that Náves does not know (a typo, or an option of a newer Náves) is rejected with the option named, in the CLI, the GUI and over MCP, instead of being read and dropped when the file is saved again.
- Lighter baked shading on the satellite images: rock faces were nearly black.

### Fixed

- The fuzz seeds that are copies of the Copernicus, ESA WorldCover and OpenStreetMap test fixtures (`dem_tile`, `imagery_tile`, `landcover_tile`, `osm_read`, `osm_ways`) carry the licences of those fixtures in `REUSE.toml`, not AGPL-3.0-or-later. Only the labels changed.
- The ground under a bridge keeps its height: the road on either side now ends flat at the abutment and its earthworks no longer spread under the span (a 25 m bridge used to have the whole gap filled to deck level).
- A bridge deck is held to the ground at its abutments, not to the valley floor under it, so a deck over a gorge stays at bank height instead of pulling the approaches down into cuttings.
- `edits/overrides.toml` with `nan` or `inf`, or with an empty or non-ASCII model name, is refused when it is read, with a message that names the object and the field (it used to reach the world file as a NaN transform, or fail late in the world writer).
- The ids of the cut ends of roads that leave the map no longer depend on which other roads cross the map edge, so edits on those roads stay attached when the OpenStreetMap data changes. **Edits made on roads at the map edge before this change lose their object once** (a warning names each); delete or redo them. An edit that replaced a model and lost its object now stands where and as turned as the object did (`generated_yaw` in `overrides.toml`, optional; older files read as 0).
- `road_grade` findings for a crest or a dip that is too sharp now say so ("vertical radius 40 m, the minimum is 60 m") instead of "Grade 1 % exceeds the limit of 0 %".
- The road bed is painted under the road pieces that were placed, not along the OpenStreetMap line, and a short OpenStreetMap bridge that was placed as ordinary road pieces gets a bed too.
- A road that ends on another now lies on the other road's ground for as far as that ground reaches (up to eight variables inward, within half a width and 1.5 cells of its centre line), instead of leaving it after the first joint. This removes the step where the side road's surface left the through road's.
- A bend is measured along the bend, not along its chord: the grade and the cross fall of an arc are read along the arc and at right angles to it, so a tight arc on a grade no longer reports part of its grade as cross fall.
- A road that ends near another road no longer takes its height when it does not lie on it: a chain end now only meets a road whose surface it is on (within its half width, or on a junction piece, whose side ports reach beyond the through road's edge), and it takes that road's height where they meet, interpolated between the joints - not the height of whatever joint happens to be within 10 m. This makes side roads level where they join, between the joints of the road they meet.
- The reported grade violations (`road_grade`) are measured on the heights that are actually used, after close roads have been brought together - not on the heights the chains had before. Most of the findings are hairpins and other places where roads that share ground cannot both keep their limits.
- A small real change in one of several overlapping images (a field, snow on a slope) no longer leaves a halo in the merged satellite image: cells are also compared with their neighbourhood.
- `naves imagery fetch` lists fetched files with a `/` in `naves.toml` on every system, so a project written on Windows builds on Linux.
- A missing imagery file is reported as missing (not as a decoding problem with a JPEG hint), with the `naves imagery fetch` command to get it again when a fetch wrote it; imagery of a large map is fetched coarser instead of failing above 16 000 pixels.
- Imagery of very different brightness (aerial photographs next to a satellite scene) is matched in brightness: the gain limits were too narrow, the change threshold could not recognise darker cells, and a gain per colour channel tinted rock green; the merge now uses one gain for all channels and keeps the colours of the sharper image.
- `naves imagery fetch` names the clearest scene and suggests other dates when every scene of the default dates is cloudy, and writes the files with the usual mode instead of a private one.
- Terrain images with real detail (photographs) no longer make `binarize` stop: `img2paa` of armake2 marked mipmaps as LZO that it had stored uncompressed, and Náves now clears the mark (armake2 itself is fixed too).
- The game client no longer crashes a few seconds after entering a generated world. The world now uses
  Livonia's grid geometry: `grid.land_size` must be exactly `grid.terrain_size / 8` (default 128; other values
  are rejected with the right value named) and the surface tiles are 8 × 8 land cells at 60 pixels per cell. The
  GUI and `project_set` derive the land grid from the terrain grid. Existing projects need `land_size` changed and
  their navigation mesh regenerated.
- The terrain no longer looks dark red in the game: the satellite and mask textures get the `AVGC`/`MAXC` (and,
  on masks, `FLAG`) tags that armake2's `img2paa` leaves out.
- The terrain shows its satellite images instead of a flat colour per tile with straight edges: the build removes
  the byte armake2's `img2paa` appends to LZO-compressed mipmaps, which the game cannot read.
- The no-data value of a GeoTIFF (for example -9999) counts as a gap in the elevation data, not as a height.
- Saving `naves.toml` keeps the file's permissions and syncs it to disk before it replaces the old file.
- A failed build keeps the mod and mission of the last good build; a new build replaces them only when it is finished.
- Relative paths in `ARMAKE2`, `PROTON` and similar variables work. External tools no longer inherit stdin, which is
  the protocol stream under `naves mcp`. On Windows, Proton is not required and Steam is found in its program folder.
- The generated mission has no gas zones at Livonia's positions. Still from Livonia: `areaflags.map` and the wildlife
  territories.
- `naves mcp`: `project_set` and `build` run one at a time, and the heightmap follows edits of `naves.toml` from
  outside. The GUI runs one heightmap job at a time and does not reload the elevation data when the name changes.
- The sun is in the right part of the sky: the world config writes the latitude with the game's sign (positive to
  the south).
- Surface masks select the right layers: grass is black and rock red at full alpha. Before, grass showed the rock
  texture and rock showed nothing.
