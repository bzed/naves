# FABDEM as the elevation source (and a look at ASTER GDEM)

Measured on 2026-11-14 on the Berchtesgaden example (`examples/berchtesgaden`, 12.8 km real extent, scale 0.5,
`terrain_size = 2048`) with the binary of commit `2e2a3e1` (`cargo build --release -p naves --locked`). Question:
would **FABDEM** (Copernicus GLO-30 with forests and buildings removed) give better roads in game than the
Copernicus GLO-30 **DSM** that Náves reads today? The maintainer's suspicion: in towns and forests the DSM includes
roofs and canopy, which bends the ground under streets.

**Short answer.** FABDEM reads without any code change and lowers the DEM-dependent road findings by about 5 to 20 %
(more in towns, hardly any in forests). It does **not** fix the roundabout on the steep street in Berchtesgaden, and
it is licensed **non-commercial and share-alike** (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0), unlike the Copernicus data. Section 6 gives the
verdict. ASTER GDEM could not be measured (no anonymous download), section 7.

Scripts, data and renders are under `/var/tmp/naves-fabdem/` (none of it is in the repository).

**What ShareAlike means for a built map.** The height data of a map built from FABDEM is derived from it: the terrain
in the `.wrp` is FABDEM resampled, smoothed and cut by road earthworks, which is "Adapted Material" in the CC text. A
map that is shared (a PBO on the Steam Workshop or any download) then has to carry its adapted material under
CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 or a compatible licence, with the attribution above, and nobody may use it commercially. Practical
consequences: (1) the licence of the whole mod must say so; (2) the maintainer cannot offer the map (or a Náves
service that outputs it) commercially, and "non-commercial" excludes anything primarily aimed at monetary
compensation (paid servers, paid Workshop items, ads); (3) other inputs may not combine well with ShareAlike
(the OSM ODbL data is not on the CC compatibility list); that is a legal question this note does not settle. This is not
legal advice.

## 2. Format, datum, grid

Checked with `gdalinfo` on `N47E012_FABDEM_V1-2.tif` and `Copernicus_DSM_COG_10_N47_00_E012_00_DEM.tif`.

| | FABDEM V1-2 | Copernicus GLO-30 (example) |
|---|---|---|
| CRS | EPSG:4326 | EPSG:4326 |
| Vertical datum | EGM2008 (EPSG:3855), per readme | EGM2008 |
| Size, pixel | 3600 x 3600, 1/3600 degree | identical |
| Origin | 11.999861 E, 48.000139 N (pixel centres on whole arc-seconds) | identical |
| `AREA_OR_POINT` | Point (was wrongly Area in V1-0) | Point |
| Type, no-data | Float32, `-9999` | Float32, no tag |
| Layout | COG, DEFLATE, predictor 2, 256 px blocks, no overviews | COG, DEFLATE, 1024 px blocks, overviews |
| Tile name | `N47E012_FABDEM_V1-2.tif` (south-west corner) | `Copernicus_DSM_COG_10_N47_00_E012_00_DEM.tif` |
| Tile size | 27.97 MB and 28.60 MB | 42.07 MB and 42.21 MB |
| Coverage | 60 degrees S to 80 degrees N | pole to pole |

The grid is the same, so the two models are cell for cell comparable here. Above 50 degrees N and below 50 degrees S
FABDEM is resampled to a uniform 1 arc-second grid while GLO-30 widens its longitude spacing; Náves reads the pixel
size per file, so this is no problem for the reader, but tiles of both sources must not be mixed in one project.
Ocean handling and polar tiles were not checked.

**Download.** The 10x10 degree zip `N40E010-N50E020_FABDEM_V1-2.zip` is 1 888 597 922 bytes (1.76 GiB). Its members are
stored without compression and the server supports range requests, so I read the zip directory and fetched only
`N47E012` and `N47E013` (about 57 MB transferred, `curl`-style user agent, no personal data). A full 10x10 zip is
up to 2.9 GB; the whole V1-2 set is 356 zips, about 0.32 TB. Berchtesgaden needs two tiles, a big map would
need a zip per 10 degrees, which an "open the right tiles" feature would have to handle.

## 3. Does Náves read it?

**Yes, unchanged.** `DemTile::from_reader` (`crates/naves-core/src/dem.rs`) wants one Float32 band in EPSG:4326, an
axis-aligned transform, and turns the `-9999` no-data value into NaN. FABDEM satisfies all of that; `sources.dem` only
had to point at the two files (`/var/tmp/naves-fabdem/project-fab/naves.toml`). No error, no warning, build exit 0.

in real metres (the game terrain has half of that, height scale 0.5).

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| whole map | 1 638 400 | -5.83 | -4.28 | -17.41 | +0.29 | -95.5 | +59.1 | 8.53 |
| tree cover | 1 004 249 | -8.96 | -8.71 | -18.60 | -0.17 | -95.5 | +44.0 | 10.62 |
| built-up | 40 766 | -1.26 | -1.05 | -4.02 | +0.63 | -13.7 | +10.3 | 2.00 |
| built-up + 50 m | 167 318 | -1.79 | -0.94 | -7.39 | +0.45 | -30.1 | +12.6 | 3.18 |
| grassland | 439 484 | -1.11 | -0.14 | -6.30 | +0.44 | -79.8 | +59.1 | 3.52 |
| bare / sparse | 92 059 | -0.07 | -0.02 | -0.57 | +0.48 | -51.8 | +19.6 | 0.94 |
| water | 49 054 | +0.03 | 0.00 | 0.00 | +0.38 | -64.5 | +31.6 | 1.38 |

FABDEM is lower almost everywhere (67 % of the map by more than 0.5 m, 59 % by more than 2 m), by about 9 m in forest
(about the canopy), 1 m in towns, and not at all on bare rock and water. In forests 87 % of the samples are lowered by
more than 2 m, so the correction there is applied nearly everywhere, as intended.

**Along the roads** (the 20 870 road objects of the Copernicus build, source difference at each position):

| roads | n | mean | median | 5 % | 95 % | RMS |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| all | 20 870 | -4.70 | -2.37 | -15.44 | +0.27 | 7.07 |
| in forest | 11 679 | -7.50 | -7.12 | -16.94 | -0.05 | 9.27 |
| in / near built-up | 10 005 | -1.52 | -0.99 | -5.46 | +0.43 | 2.58 |
| other | 2 084 | -1.37 | -0.25 | -6.78 | +0.41 | 2.87 |

(The classes overlap because built-up +50 m and forest touch.) The final built terrain differs by -2.92 m on average
over the map (median -2.12, RMS 4.36) and by -2.27 m (median -1.16) at the road objects; that is the source difference
times 0.5 plus different earthworks.

**Bumpiness** (mean absolute 3x3 Laplacian residual of the 10 m source, in metres, Copernicus then FABDEM): built-up
+50 m 0.198 / 0.156 (-21 %), cropland 0.114 / 0.080, tree cover 0.258 / 0.261 (unchanged), grassland 0.270 / 0.291,
bare 0.358 / 0.364. So FABDEM does smooth the towns, but not the forests: it lowers the canopy, it does not remove its
roughness.

## 6. Verdict

- **Does FABDEM fix road findings?** A little, and mostly in town. All DEM-dependent counts drop (misses above 2 m
  -21 %, grade -13 %, not drivable length -17 %, largest miss 20 m to 10 m), towns by about -17 to -28 %, forests by
  -3 to -12 % with some worse. The confused roundabout stays confused: its layout comes from the 6 m cells, the OSM
  geometry and the road solver, not from the roof bias. FABDEM is not the main cause of the findings.
- **What it costs.** (1) The licence: non-commercial and ShareAlike on the resulting terrain, with the Copernicus
  statement; Copernicus GLO-30 allows free use with attribution. (2) Distribution: a 1.8 GB zip per 10 degrees, no
  per-tile files, but the stored zip allows ranged reads of single tiles. (3) Accuracy: the paper reports mean absolute
  vertical error in built-up areas going from 1.61 m to 1.12 m and in forests from 5.15 m to 2.88 m against reference
  data (12 countries); it is a machine-learned estimate, not a surveyed DTM, so 3 m remain in forests and its artefacts
  are not predictable. (4) Coverage ends at 60 degrees S and 80 degrees N. (5) Heights are 9 m lower in forests;
  nothing breaks, but cut and fill change (max cut 41 to 49 m).
- **Fit for Náves.** Technically free (no reader change). If it is offered, it needs a **project setting for a
  non-commercial elevation source**, mirroring imagery: an entry like `sources.dem_attribution` /
  `dem_licence` / `dem_non_commercial` (or turning `sources.dem` into a list of tables like `sources.imagery`) so that
  the build can put the attribution into the mod and refuse or warn when the project is declared commercial. Today
  `non_commercial` exists for imagery only (`crates/naves-core/src/project.rs`, `docs/manual/src/project-file.md`).
  Whether it is worth it is the maintainer's call: the gain in this measurement is about 10 % fewer findings for a
  licence that limits what can be done with every map.

## 7. ASTER GDEM V3

**Not measured: no anonymous source.** What was verified:

- Official release text (Japan Space Systems, 6 August 2019,
  `https://www.jspacesystems.or.jp/ersdac/GDEM/ver3Validation/GDEM3_Announcement_en.pdf`): 1 arc-second (30 m) GeoTIFF
  tiles of 1 degree, 83 degrees N to 83 degrees S, WGS84 / **EGM96** geoid, "accuracies ... are 20 meters at 95 %
  confidence for vertical data and 30 meters at 95 % confidence for horizontal data", available "at no charge to users
  worldwide". It is a stereo-photogrammetric surface model (canopy and buildings in it), assembled from stacked scenes;
  version 3 reduced voids and water anomalies. The vertical accuracy is several times worse than Copernicus'.
- Terms: NASA's data use page (`https://lpdaac.usgs.gov/data/data-citation-and-policies/` (redirects to the NASA Earthdata "Data Use and Citation Guidance")) says that unless marked with a restriction or licence,
  data from NASA-led missions are CC0 and citation is strongly urged. ASTER is a joint METI/NASA product; I did not
  find a licence statement for it on the pages I could read, so "free for any use" is **not verified**.
- Access: LP DAAC (Earthdata Search, Data Pool, AppEEARS) works with an Earthdata login, and Japan Space Systems asks for
  a registration. My unauthenticated requests to the Data Pool paths returned 404, not a tile. OpenTopography's public
  bucket `opentopography.s3.sdsc.edu/raster/` can be listed anonymously but holds COP30, COP90, NASADEM, SRTM, AW3D30,
  `EU_DTM` and `GEDTM30` and no ASTER; its API answers 401 without a key. I created no account and used no credentials,
  as instructed, so no tile was downloaded.

**Combination (reasoned, not measured).** With a stated 20 m vertical error at 95 % and typical pits and bumps,
ASTER cannot improve Copernicus (specified at a few metres) in a place that has no voids, and it has the same canopy and
roof bias; averaging or cross-checking would add its noise. A fill for Copernicus voids is the only sensible use, and
GLO-30 has few of them. If a bare-earth source is the goal, the candidates found on the open OpenTopography bucket are
worth a look next (`GEDTM30`, a global terrain model, and `EU_DTM`); their licences were not checked. What the data
here shows is that the useful combination would be FABDEM inside towns (where it smooths) and Copernicus elsewhere,
but that gives the licence cost of FABDEM on the whole map for the gain in towns alone.

## 8. Limits of this note

One map, one scale, one area in the Alps. Road placement is solved per build, so single counts are not a precise
yardstick (+-5 % between sources may be noise of the solver; the repeat run of Copernicus was exactly equal, so there is no
run-to-run noise). Nothing was tested in game. The land cover tile is a 10 m raster of 2021 and misclassifies some
roads. The along-road statistics use the Copernicus road objects as positions for both sources.

### Licence (verified from the official source)

The "Distribution of ASTER GDEM" page of Japan Space Systems (`https://www.jspacesystems.or.jp/ersdac/GDEM/E/3.html`,
Japanese `.../J/3.html`) states: version 2 was restricted to GEOSS societal-benefit uses; "**In version 3, it will become
public domain, so anyone can freely use it.**" The Japanese page is more explicit: "ASTER GDEM and ASTWBD are public
domain. Redistribution and commercial use are completely fine." Free of charge. Credit is a request, not a condition:
"ASTER GDEM (or ASTWBD) courtesy of METI and NASA". Disclaimer: data are "as is" and contain anomalies and artefacts.
The JPL page (`https://asterweb.jpl.nasa.gov/gdem.asp`) says "available at no charge for any user pursuant to an agreement
between METI and NASA". So "free for any use since version 3" is **confirmed**, with the caveat that the Tellus mirror
adds its own terms.

### Datum and format

1 arc-second GeoTIFF, 1 degree tiles of 3601 x 3601 px (edges shared), signed 16 bit, 1 m per count, WGS84 / **EGM96**
geoid, void `-9999`, 83 N to 83 S, stated accuracy 20 m (95 %) vertical (release announcement, 6 August 2019; tile layout
as documented for the sibling ASTWBD product, not checked on a real tile). Náves does not model the geoid; EGM96 and
EGM2008 differ by decimetres to about a metre in the Alps, a smooth offset. To mix ASTER with Copernicus it has to be
converted first, e.g. `gdalwarp -s_srs EPSG:4326+5773 -t_srs EPSG:4326+3855` (needs the PROJ geoid grids), and the
edge-sharing tile resampled onto the Copernicus grid. Not done here for lack of data.

### Combination with Copernicus (reasoned, not measured)

A stereo-photogrammetric surface model with a stated 20 m error cannot improve Copernicus (a few metres) where Copernicus
has data, and it carries the same canopy and roof bias, so a **blend or average adds noise** and a cross-check would only
flag ASTER's own artefacts. The one useful role is a **void fill** for Copernicus no-data cells; Copernicus has none in
Berchtesgaden (0 void pixels in both tiles). **Verdict: Copernicus alone beats any combination here.**

### AW3D30 (JAXA), a separate candidate, not measured

JAXA's AW3D30 (v4.1, April 2024, 1 arc-second DSM, `https://www.eorc.jaxa.jp/ALOS/en/dataset/aw3d30/aw3d30_e.htm`) is
free for commercial and non-commercial use "under JAXA's Terms for Use" (`https://earth.jaxa.jp/policy/en.html`, not read
in full; attribution not verified), but **needs a registered account** (form plus e-mail confirmation,
`https://www.eorc.jaxa.jp/ALOS/en/aw3d30/registration.htm`). It is a surface model like Copernicus, with the same
canopy and roof bias; GEDTM30 below uses it as an input.

## GEDTM30

Measured on the same project setup (`/var/tmp/naves-dem2/project-gedtm`, same binary, same 2048 settings, same headless
sway), compared with Copernicus and the FABDEM numbers above. The Copernicus and FABDEM columns are the earlier runs; the
extraction tool for the final elevation and objects reproduced the FABDEM files byte for byte.

| CRS, datum | EPSG:4326; vertical **EGM2008 (EPSG:3855)** (tag `VERTICAL_DATUM=EGM2008/EPSG:3855`), same as Copernicus |
| Grid | 1 arc-second, **1 296 009 x 540 009 px**, origin (-180.00125, 85.00125), `AREA_OR_POINT=Area`; pixel centres on whole arc-seconds, i.e. **cell for cell identical to Copernicus GLO-30** (a shift test against the Copernicus tile gave the smallest difference at zero shift) |
| Type, no-data | Float32, no-data `3.4028235e+38`; COG, DEFLATE, predictor 2, 2048 px blocks, overviews. Values are metres (369 to 3670 m here) although the file carries a GDAL **`Scale: 0.1`** tag; a reader that applied the tag would be off by a factor ten, one that ignores it (Náves, `gdal_translate` raw copy) is right |
| Coverage | 65 S to 85 N, no Antarctica; no void pixels in the window |
| Published accuracy | RMSE against lidar: tree cover over 50 % 3.73 m (Copernicus 5.14), built-up 3.03 m (4.06); aggregated reference sites built-up 2.75 m, stable tree cover 6.72 m (FABDEM 2.74 and 6.82); Trentino (Alps) 7.09 m vs FABDEM 8.93 and FathomDEM 5.45; against GNSS stations 10.69 m. Stated limitations: uncertainty highest in dense forest and the big mountain ranges **including the Alps**, "slightly noisier/softer" than FABDEM in Trentino, sometimes overestimates steep slopes and underestimates high altitude, some tree patches remain, lidar reference sites under-represent forest |

### Window download and reading it

No download of the global file: `gdal_translate -projwin` through `/vsicurl/` (generic user agent, no credentials) with
the Copernicus tile bounds `11.9998611 48.0001389 12.9998611 47.0001389` (and the E013 neighbour) wrote two
3600 x 3600 Float32 GeoTIFFs of 30.6 MB and 31.1 MB. **About 96.7 MB were fetched over 37 range requests** for both tiles
(the 2048 px COG blocks add overhead). The output is a plain GeoTIFF.

**Does Náves read it unchanged?** Yes, once the window is cut. `sources.dem` pointed at the two tiles, build exit 0, no
error, no DEM warning. What is *not* unchanged: the global COG is 1 296 009 px wide, far above `MAX_SIDE` (16 384) and
`MAX_PIXELS` in `crates/naves-core/src/dem.rs`, so a window has to be cut first (or the reader would have to read
windows of a COG over HTTP, an "open the right area" feature). A reader that honours GDAL's scale tag would break on it.

### Verdict for GEDTM30

- **Licence: the best of the bare-earth candidates.** CC BY 4.0 allows commercial use and has no share-alike, so it does
  not need the `non_commercial` machinery that FABDEM would.
- **Technically:** it reads unchanged once a tile-sized window is cut (about 97 MB over HTTP range requests for this
  map); grid and datum are identical to Copernicus.
- **Quality for roads:** in towns it matches FABDEM (large misses and non-drivable lengths drop by 21 to 28 %); over the
  whole map the DEM-dependent counts fall only 5 to 11 % (FABDEM 9 to 13 %), and in forests and in the open steep paths
  (+14 %), capped earthworks (+24 %) and grade get worse because the model is noisy there (its published accuracy is
  weakest in mountains, too). It is labelled "use for testing purposes only".
- **Combination (not built):** the per-class numbers say the useful form would be GEDTM30 **only inside built-up zones**
  (WorldCover class 50 plus a margin) with a feathered edge, Copernicus elsewhere. That needs a blend step in the reader
  or a pre-processing tool; I did not test it.
